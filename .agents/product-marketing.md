# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-08-09

## Portfolio Overview

**One-liner:** A personal portfolio for Luke Cassady-Dorion: apps, tools, technical explanations, and films.

**What it does:** The site introduces Luke's independent work and his full-time developer-relations practice. It gives prospective collaborators, employers, and clients enough context to understand the range of his work, then points them to the work itself.

**Category:** Personal portfolio.

**Business model:** No product pricing or sales funnel. The desired outcome is a relevant conversation, collaboration, or hiring inquiry.

## Audience

**Primary audiences:** Prospective collaborators, employers, clients, and people discovering Luke's work through search.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Understand what Luke makes without forcing him into an inaccurate job title.
- See evidence across software, developer relations, documentation, music, and film.
- Find a direct way to begin a relevant conversation.

## Positioning

**Positioning:** Luke builds apps, tools, technical explanations, and films. The work is the proof; the site should describe it plainly rather than use aspirational labels.

**Differentiation:** A connected body of work across independent software, technical education, documentation systems, and moving-image work.

## Customer Language

**Words to use:** Apps, tools, technical explanations, developer relations, documentation, films, independent work, collaboration.

**Words to avoid:** Designer, maker, creative technologist, innovation, seamless, cutting-edge, generic superlatives, and vague mission statements.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Plain, thoughtful, specific, and unforced.

**Style:** First person where useful; short declarative sentences; show the work rather than inflate it.

**Personality:** Curious, capable, independent, grounded.

## Proof Points

**Selected evidence:** Sow for iPhone and iPad; developer-relations explainers and documentation for DFlow; released music; documentary and brand-film work.

**Evidence rule:** Do not invent metrics, outcomes, titles, or credentials. Add concrete results only when Luke can verify them.

## Goals

**Primary goal:** Make the portfolio understandable to people and search systems while keeping the visual experience distinct.

**Conversion action:** Contact Luke about relevant work or collaboration.

## Working Assumptions To Confirm

- The portfolio should foreground work categories, not a single professional title.
- The Three.js studies and assembly pages are internal/prototype surfaces and should not be indexed.
- Future project pages should provide the durable SEO depth; the homepage should remain a concise overview.
